Grandpa Kadel's Scrapple

⏱️ Prep: 20 mins
🍳 Cook: ~30 mins
❄️ Chill: Until Solid

Ingredients

The Meat & Seasoning

  • 1 pound sausage
  • 1 tbsp sage (optional, to taste)
  • 1 tsp salt (optional, to taste)
  • ½ tsp black pepper (optional, to taste)

The Mash & Frying

  • 5 cups water (divided: 3 cups for blending, 2 cups for cooking)
  • 2½ cups plain white corn meal (not self-rising)
  • As needed butter or oil (for frying and serving)

Instructions

  1. Brown the Meat: Brown and crumble 1 pound of sausage in a heavy skillet. If your sausage isn't already seasoned to your liking, add the 1 tablespoon of sage, 1 teaspoon of salt, and ½ teaspoon of black pepper while cooking.
  2. Blend: Once cooked and browned, transfer the sausage into a blender along with 3 cups of water. Blend for 10 to 15 seconds, just to break down larger lumps but not to puree.
  3. Thicken the Mash: Pour the blended mixture into a large saucepan. Stir in the remaining 2 cups of water and 2½ cups of plain white corn meal. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, until it thickens and boils slowly (like a farting pool of lava).
  4. Steam: Turn the heat down to very low, cover the pan with a lid, and let it cook for an additional 15 minutes.
  5. Mold and Chill: Pour the hot mixture into a loaf pan. Don't worry there is a brown layer stuck to the bottom of the pot, that's normal. Refrigerate until completely cooled and solid.
  6. Portion: Once solidified, turn it out, cut it into 3 bricks, and wrap them individually for storage.
  7. To Serve: Slice the bricks into square patties and fry them in a skillet with butter or oil until crispy on the outside.
A Note from Grandpa, Robert B. Kadel, who worked out this recipe by trial and error:
"This recipe was developed by slaves in the Old South to make use of the parts of the pig left over after slaughter. After the Civil War, it was taken north by the freed slaves as they migrated, and it became known as Philadelphia Scrapple."
